I Book One · The personal Out now

Stone Breath

A love story about roots, wholeness, and a success that doesn’t cost your soul.

A Diné man arrives in a Lithuanian village carrying his grandfather’s book wrapped in a storm-pattern blanket — and a life that looks like success and feels like nothing. There he meets Austėja, a mountain runner who hands him back exactly what his own great-grandmother taught him, in another language, on the other side of the world.

It is carried to a hundred-mile night race across a high Alpine pass — and the sentences are shaped like breathing, with a real tool sitting inside every turn of the story.

By Lamplight

Masan’s lessons, from an ancient homestead to the world stage.

The formation story: how a boy raised on Big Mountain by his great-grandmother carried five ancestral voices out of a hogan and onto the global stage. Two strands run side by side — the camp then, and the world now; the raising, and what it produced.

The key book of the sage, and likely the widest-reaching book in the series.

III Book Three · The civilizational Out now

The Council Fire

The original instructions for a breaking world.

The world it opens in is the one outside your window. The fires that come earlier every year. The storms we run out of letters to name. And underneath the weather, a quieter worry — the sense that the systems built to hold us have stopped being able to think past the next quarter.

It begins there. Not in despair, but in a circle of people who have watched worlds end before, and remember what a person keeps when the lights go out.

  • IIThe Keepers — the circle gathers; many nations, one fire.

The Far Messengers

A record of seven days at the World Congress on Human Continuity.

A near future where the ice is gone. Two featured speakers — a song keeper from the Baltic and a navigator from the Pacific — carry the oldest human knowledge onto the highest stage there is: a body chartered to think past the term of any government.

Told through narration, three podcast broadcasts, the official sessions, and the private rooms between them.

  • Rasa VaitkutėA Lithuanian song keeper of Gabija’s line; keeps the sutartinės; eleven desert crossings as her training ground.
  • Hanohano Kealoha-NakamuraNative Hawaiian and Japanese; a channel-swimming champion taught the star compass and swell-reading the old way.

From “What the Sand Taught the Songs”

On the fourth day, the well was dry. He came down off the last dune to the place the water should have been, and the seep was cracked mud, pale, and there was nothing.

A frightened man does one thing here, and it is always the same thing. His heart bolts. And they find him the next morning a few hours’ walk away with the panic still on his face, and the well he needed sitting half a day north of where he died.

This man did not do that. He sat down. He let the fear stand there beside him and gave it no chair to sit in. And then his heart came down. And when it did, his quiet mind did the thing his racing mind could never have done. It remembered.
