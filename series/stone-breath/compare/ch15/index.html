The Land Covenant

The Land Covenant

The next morning Tonas took them down to the creek. "Where I come from, you don't just live on land," he said. "You're in relationship with it. My great-grandmother taught me that. So when I settled here, I made a covenant with this water." He knelt, cupped his hands, drank, then sprinkled a few drops back. "Three questions. And you can make this covenant with any ground that holds you — a creek, a park, a patch of dirt behind your building. It travels." He looked at Austėja. "Am I taking only what is given?" She nodded. "Am I leaving enough for the next season?" She nodded again. "Am I offering something in return?" He looked at Ugnė. "You." Ugnė stepped forward. "What do I offer?" "A promise. That you will remember this place. That you will tell your children about it. That you will never take without giving back." Ugnė knelt, cupped her hands, drank, then sprinkled water back into the creek. "I promise." Tonas touched her shoulder. "Then the covenant is made. The land knows you now."

The Canyons

The Canyons

He gave them the country the way he had been given it. They hiked West Fork one afternoon, the trail crossing and re-crossing the creek a dozen times, the canyon walls leaning in until the sky was a ribbon overhead and the water ran so clear you could count the stones on the bottom. At the deep pool where the forks met they swam, and Ugnė climbed a low ledge and jumped and came up howling, and something in Tonas's chest cracked open — because this was the water he had played in at her age, and here she was — not his by blood, not yet by any document, but by every measure that had ever counted to him — in it too.

The Mesa Run
