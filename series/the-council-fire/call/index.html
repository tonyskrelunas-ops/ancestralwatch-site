The Council Fire · a call

The Council Fire: a call.

Why this book exists, and what it asks of us now. By Tony Skrelūnas.

To the relatives. To the keepers. To everyone carrying more than their share as the ground changes under us.

Where this came from

This book began in a dream I kept having. Not a gentle wish. A solemn, recurring thing that arrived with the weight of prophecy. In it I walked through a world both familiar and shattered, broken by a single flawed operating system, an economics of extraction and a technology of domination, and then, out of the silence, a council fire was lit.

I was led into a great round room beneath a vast starry sky, and I took a seat among my ancestors. Hopi dry farmers. Sámi reindeer herders. Peruvian healers, Māori navigators, the keepers of deserts and forests and steppes. Scientists and engineers sat among us as humble listeners. Our whole charge was to weigh every new thing that wanted to be built against one question:

Will this repeat the cycles of collapse, or does it honor the generations not yet born?

I have been awake inside that question ever since. I was created for this purpose. And what I was shown, over and over, was the rebuilding. Not the breaking. The fire already lit, and the room full of people who had come through.

The hard ground

We felt it first in the wind. The winds turned volatile, arriving off their old calendar, gusting from the wrong quarter, no longer the steady relatives we had known. We felt it in the rain. The soft female rains, the gentle ones that come walking slow and sink all the way in, grew rare.

The numbers say the same thing in their own language. In November 2026 the Climate Action Tracker projected about 2.7°C of warming above pre-industrial levels by 2100 under the policies in place today, and about 2.1°C even if every pledge and long-term target is met.

Which means the crossing has come to us. Not to a rare few. To all of us. We are all the far messenger now: the ones out on the trail as the signs come in.

What the keepers teach

In the book, twelve keepers on six continents, none of whom has heard the others speak, each hold one small thing their people carried through a breaking: a lump of coal, an amber bead, a seed, a river stone worn smooth by many thumbs. No one people was ever given the whole of it to carry alone. At the end, the keepers were meant to find one another.

Gather the keepers. We were never meant to do this alone.

They sit with no head to the table. And they learn what every far messenger knew: no one reads a hard thing from inside a frightened body. A council is only as wise as the nervous systems that compose it.

Not every place breaks at once. Some will keep their rains, their cooler air, their water, and those become the arks, where the old ways and the wildlife and the living climate hold on. The healing begins there and spreads outward, the messengers carrying it from the arks back into the returning lands.

The call

So this is what I am asking of you, wherever you are.

  1. Light a council fire where you live. Your family, your team, your town. A circle, with no head to the table.
  2. Settle the body first. The stone in the hand, the counted breath, four in and six out, until the body stands down. Then decide.
  3. Gather the keepers. Find the elders and the ones who still hold the songs, the seeds, the skills and the ways of a place, while they are still here. Sit with them. Feed them.
  4. Ask the one question of what you build, buy and choose: will this repeat the cycles of collapse, or does it honor the generations not yet born?
  5. Stand with the arks. Protect the places that still keep their water, their forests and their rains, and stand with the peoples who have kept them.
  6. Pass it on. Hand to hand, the way it came to us.

Come. Sit at the fire. There is room. There is always room.

Ahéhee’,
Tony Skrelūnas
