The Council Fire · A Call

A Call from the Council Fire

The Original Instructions for the World We Rebuild. A message to the human family, November 2026.

From Tony Skrelūnas, author of The Ancestral Watch

Listen · 10 min

9:45

In brief

The world is entering another crossing. The heat arrives too early, the prices lurch, the storms carry a strange new weight, and most of us are meeting it in fight or flight.

This call does not ask for a new plan. It asks us to pick up the instructions our ancestors already tested, on every continent and in every language, and to use them again:

  • Ask one question of everything before it is built.
  • Count wealth in four kinds, not one.
  • Make four repairs, beginning with the land.
  • Govern in the circle, close to home.
  • Own things for the great-grandchildren.
  • Share knowledge freely, and treat the gathering as the infrastructure of trust.
  • Raise people whole enough to cross.

These are not inventions. They are rememberings.

Relatives, leaders, keepers, and every member of our human family

We are entering a time of global uncertainty. Most of us are living in fight or flight. In urgency. In a noise so constant that we have lost the ability to sit with a story long enough for it to change anything in us.

You already feel it coming. You scroll the news at night and feel the world tipping, and feel yourself tipping with it. You are carrying too much — a family, a job, a grief, a world — and no one ever taught you that the load was never meant to be carried alone.

I will not make it smaller than it is. What is breaking is a single flawed operating system: an economics of extraction and a technology of domination. But there is a path through. It has been walked before.

Where this call comes from

The reason I started this writing was the global climate crisis, and my own dreams and visions of what is to come. The hard grounds. And that I was created for this purpose.

The Creator guides everything in my life. Every book, every story, every article.

In 2020 I put the dream in a letter to an old friend in Peru:

I have had dreams in the past that we will survive through a crazy period when climate change really escalates and speeds up the world’s disasters. In my dreams it happens fast. There is a post-apocalypse elder council that I am on that now reviews new approaches and technologies, to make sure that they are used for the right reason and that we won’t repeat the mistakes of the past. I’m not sure what that means, but maybe that we are on the right track.

I have written it down many times over since. The Council Fire began in that dream I kept having. In it I walked through a world both familiar and shattered, and then, out of the silence, a council fire was lit.

I was led into a great round room beneath a vast starry sky, and I took a seat among my ancestors. Hopi dry farmers. Sámi reindeer herders. Peruvian healers, Māori navigators, the keepers of deserts and forests and steppes. Scientists and engineers sat among us as humble listeners.

The council had one charge: to weigh every new thing that wanted to be built against one question.

Will this repeat the cycles of collapse, or does it honor the generations not yet born?

I have been awake inside that question ever since. This call is that question, carried out of the book and set down in front of all of us.

The hard ground

We felt it first in the wind. The winds turned volatile, arriving off their old calendar, gusting from the wrong quarter, no longer the steady relatives we had known, but something restless and stranger every year.

We felt it in the rain. The soft female rains, the gentle ones that come walking slow and sink all the way in, that the corn drinks for days, grew rare. In their place came the hard male rains: too much at once, drenching and violent, running off the baked ground and carrying the topsoil away down the wash. Anyone who farms with their hands knows the difference between a rain that feeds and a rain that wounds. Everywhere on the earth, the water had stopped keeping its appointments.

The numbers say the same thing in their own language. In November 2026 the Climate Action Tracker projected about 2.7°C of warming above pre-industrial levels by 2100 under the policies in place today, and about 2.1°C even if every pledge and long-term target is met.

We are entering a time when we must be resilient to cross: the hard deserts, the waves and oceans bigger than ever. And then the rebuilding.

What I was shown

What I was shown, over and over, was the rebuilding. Not the breaking. The fire already lit, and the room full of people who had come through.

A window. A single light. In the worst winter the world had known. And then another, and another: small lights scattered across the dark of the whole earth. A hogan in the high desert, smoke rising. A stone croft on a snowed hill. A butter lamp in a monastery above the failing snows. A stilt house over the black rice-water. A low farmhouse in coal country, its pumps still turning. And a fire on a hill, and a song carrying across the dark.

The light was the point. Everything else, the collapse, the hunger, the silence, was just the story of what broke. The light was the story of what held.

And the keepers did the thing that was the whole difference between an ark and a bunker. They opened the door. They fed the ones who came from the cellar that the old way had filled. They warmed them at the fire. And then they taught them.

Which means the crossing has come to you. Not to a rare few. To all of us. We are all the far messenger now: the ones out on the trail as the signs come in.

It has been walked before

For most of human history, everything that mattered moved inside a trained human being. The far messengers crossed the deadly distances carrying what the people needed, and delivered it whole. Knowledge, stories, songs, medicine, seed, the way across: all of it rode inside people who lived a certain way, settled in the body and clear in the mind, so the cargo arrived undamaged. Every people on earth raised them. Every one of us has them in our line.

The way out of a breaking world is not a theory either. The community that owns its own sun instead of renting it. The wealth measured in four kinds of capital instead of one. The cooperative that grew from a town with no work into eighty thousand owners. The council that asks one question of everything it builds.

These are not inventions. They are rememberings. They have been tested, in real communities, on four continents, by people who would have died if they got it wrong.

The Original Instructions

What follows is carried word for word from the back of The Council Fire, as the council left it. Under each one is what I am asking of us.

1.The One Question (the Sacred Veto)

Before anything is built, funded, or deployed, the elders ask: Will this repeat the cycles of collapse, or does it honor the great-grandchildren? If the answer is no, it does not happen. Not delayed. Stopped.

What I askAsk it out loud in every room where something is about to be built, funded, or deployed: the council chamber, the boardroom, the budget meeting, the kitchen table.

2.The Four Capitals

A thing is wealthy only if it grows four kinds of capital at once — financial (the money), relational (trust and kinship), cultural (language, songs, ceremonies, seeds), and natural (water, soil, the living land). A venture that grows the first by shrinking the other three is not a success. It is a thief.

What I askCount all four, and report all four. Refuse the venture that grows money by spending down trust, culture, or the land.

3.The Four Repairs

(1) Clean the land first — restoration as the first economy, owned by the wounded. (2) Let the sun replace the coal — power owned by the people, not rented to strangers. (3) Build the middle — a web of small owned enterprises and a community bank, not one big employer. (4) Make the land a destination — heritage and encounter as the wealth that never depletes.

What I askBegin with the land, and let the people who were wounded own the healing of it.

4.How They Governed

Leaders recognized, not campaigned for. Power held close — the local council, not the distant capital. Elders and culture-keepers at the center, holding the Sacred Veto. Decisions woven in the circle until they hold the best thinking of everyone, not voted to a winning half. And the tools always serve the circle; they never become it.

What I askHold decisions close to the people who will live with them, keep the elders and the keepers at the center, and let every tool, the newest machines included, serve the circle.

5.What They Owned

Companies chartered so they cannot be sold, their mission locked at the level of their bones, a fixed share of their revenue flowing off the top into the Great-Grandchildren’s Fund — never the body of it spent, only the draw, and the draw paid out before anything else is reckoned — value circling home to the seeds, the songs, and the healing of the land.

What I askIf you build or fund a company, lock its mission into its bones, and send a fixed share off the top to the great-grandchildren before anything else is counted.

6.The New Amber Road

Trade as interdependence, not competition. The highest good traded is knowledge, shared freely. Celebration — the gathering, the fire — is not a frill but the load-bearing infrastructure of trust.

What I askShare what you know without a toll on it, and fund the gathering and the fire as seriously as you fund the road.

Underneath all of it

Underneath all of it is a single aim, and it is not survival. It is to raise people whole enough to cross — settled in the body, clear in the mind, able to carry what matters through a hard passage and arrive with it undamaged. That is what the healing is for. Not comfort. Capacity.

This is the work of two generations at once: ours, and the one that comes after. So that the ones who inherit this are finally healed and genuinely resilient, and when it is their turn to build, they build from knowledge instead of from panic.

So here is what I ask of each of us. Begin on an ordinary day. Hold a stone. Count the breath: in for four, hold for four, out for six, hold for four. You can only spend, in the emergency, what you already stored.

Find a keeper — someone who holds a song, a craft, a language, three hundred plants, and has been told it is worth nothing — and feed them. Sit with them. Learn one thing from them while they are here to teach it.

And when the size of it all makes you small, remember what the navigator says near the end of the book.

Teach one kid. Fix the fence. Feed people. Put the knowledge in a hand.
It was never the big structure. It was somebody who knew how to do something, standing next to somebody who wanted to learn.

What will you set into the ground?

The collapse is the story of what broke. The fire on the hill — the lit window in the dark, the door standing open — is the story of what held. Both of them are true, and you will need both.
When the bowl shatters — and it always shatters — what will you have set into the ground before it does?

Come. Sit at the fire. There is room. There is always room.

Tony Skrelūnas
The Ancestral Watch · November 2026

Carry this call. Send it to one person who is carrying too much, or to someone who sits in a room where things get built.
