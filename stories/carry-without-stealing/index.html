Ideas & Framework

How to carry a tradition without stealing it.

The method underneath the whole body of work.

Listen · 3 min

2:41

The modern wellness economy has a signature failure, and it is not that the tools don't work. It is that it sells the messengers' tools with the messengers' world deleted — no circle, no elders, no line, no one who will tell you the truth about yourself. A breath technique performed alone in an apartment, stripped of the container it grew in, will help you tolerate a life that should not be tolerated. That is the extraction. It is quieter than the theft of land, and it is the same shape.

The usual responses both fail. One says the old knowledge was superstition — discard the frame, keep the "science." The other says no knowledge is truer than any other, so touch nothing. The first plunders. The second freezes. Neither lets a living person actually pick up a practice their own ancestors used.

There is a third position, and it is the only honest one from which this work can be done. Call it what it is: metamodernist. The ancestral practices were tested — ten thousand years of field trials with survival as the endpoint. The laboratory findings are real. Neither has to defeat the other. Fiction can carry working instruction. A ceremony can be described by its effect without disclosing its content. A practice can be honored and examined at the same time.

Take the timeless principle from the time-bound practice — and adapt it to your own life. You will never be asked to mimic a tradition that isn't yours.

In practice it comes down to a few disciplines. Extract the principle, not the costume: the long exhale that quiets the nervous system is human physiology; the specific ceremony it sat inside is not yours to perform. When a tool traces to a particular people, name the hand it came from, or universalize it honestly — never both blurred together. What is sacred and closed stays closed; you describe its effect, never its content. And you submit the work to the people whose thread it touches before the public sees it, and where they draw a line, you honor it.

The shield in all of this is not a disclaimer. It is a fact: a birthright cannot be appropriated, because every people on earth was once indigenous somewhere. The runner, the wayfinder, the grandmother at the lamp — some version of them stands at the far end of your line too. You are not being handed someone else's inheritance. You are being pointed back toward your own.
