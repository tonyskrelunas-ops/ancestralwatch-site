**My grandfather kept his songs in a language I was losing.**

I'm Skrelūnas — Lithuanian on one side, Diné (Navajo) on the other. Two peoples the world worked very hard to make forget who they were. And for a long time it was working on me. The *dainos* my family carried got quieter with each generation. The amber sat in a drawer. I could feel the thread thinning.

A few years ago I did something about it. I took what my elders gave me — the songs, the old way of seeing the land as alive, the belief that the people who came before you aren't gone but standing behind you — and I carried it back. Then I put it into stories, so my kids and grandkids could never lose it the way I almost did.

That's what the *Ancestral Watch* books are. Not fantasy. A map home.

The first one, **Stone Breath**, is about a Lithuanian-Navajo man who takes his grandfather's songs back to the old homeland — and meets a woman who is already whole. If you've ever felt caught between worlds — an old country you can only half-remember and a new one that never quite fit — this one was written for you.

I'm not posting this to sell anything. I'm posting because I think many of us here carry the same quiet ache, and I want you to know the thread isn't broken. It can be picked back up.

If you're curious, the books — and a free 3-minute tool drawn from them — are here: **ancestralwatch.com/lt**

*Ačiū.* Walk in beauty. 🌿
